## Five outputs that should not be mixed together

| Output | What it means | What it does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| Observed native percentile | Standing among same-role peers in one completed season | Permanent talent or career value |
| Native forecast | Experimental next-season role percentile | Guaranteed future performance |
| Physical estimate | Association between listed build and target-role outcomes | A causal effect of body type |
| Body fit | How central the build is in the target-role distribution | Playing ability |
| Transfer analogy | Resemblance through build and shared themes | Probability of changing sports successfully |

## Appropriate uses

- demonstrating a reproducible multi-sport analytics workflow;
- exploring broad athlete-role archetypes;
- finding cases worth qualitative film or coaching review;
- showing how validation and abstention affect a public-facing model;
- generating research questions about transferable physical and playing profiles.

## Uses the project cannot support

- contracts, roster selection or draft decisions by itself;
- medical, injury or safety decisions;
- claims that a particular athlete would succeed after changing sports;
- rankings across roles as if an 80 in every role represented equal sporting value;
- conclusions about awareness, reaction speed, intelligence or motivation.

## Important assumptions

The project assumes broad roles are stable enough for first-pass comparison, opportunity is a reasonable measure of evidence strength, and within-role percentiles reduce some season and provider drift.

Those assumptions can be useful without being perfectly true. They remain visible so future work can test them.

## Public-data limitations

Public summaries omit tactics, off-ball assignments, coaching intent and many tracking events. Physical measurements often lack dates and standardized testing protocols. Premier League physical coverage is still materially lower than the other sports.

## Static-site release boundary

This portfolio edition contains a curated set of derived results. It does not publish raw source files, processed player-season rows, saved models, coefficients, provider imagery, or league and team marks.

Every static JSON file should be treated as publicly downloadable. Expanding from curated examples to all players requires a separate rights and attribution review.

## Highest-value future improvements

1. Acquire rights-cleared tracking, testing and dated physical measurements.
2. Validate narrower tactical roles where lineup evidence permits.
3. Design prospective tests with coaches and sport scientists.
4. Collect real transition outcomes rather than relying only on analogy.
5. Continue reporting uncertainty and withholding weak answers.
