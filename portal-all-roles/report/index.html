Research journey · snapshot dated 21 October 2026

From four leagues to one careful comparison.

This project asks whether an athlete’s listed build and proven style of play resemble the profiles of roles in another sport. It is a research aid for finding interesting comparisons—not a promise that someone can switch sports.

See what we found Understand the scores
The question changed as the project grew.

It began with possession-winning players: defenders, interceptors, pressure creators and disruptors.

The current version covers broad roles across the NFL, NBA, NHL and Premier League so those defensive archetypes can be seen in the context of an entire league.

The research question

What does “fit” mean here?

The project separates questions that can sound similar but need very different evidence. A player can perform well in their own role, look physically typical for another role, or share transferable-looking performance traits. None of those alone proves they could change sports.

1

Describe the athlete

Place a player’s recent performance in the context of athletes who played the same broad role in the same season.

2

Compare the build

Ask whether listed height, weight, body-mass index and age resemble the range seen in a target role.

3

Form a transfer hypothesis

Combine build with the performance themes that both sports can reasonably share, then report a cautious analogy score.

Important boundary: the result is not the probability of making a roster, learning new technique, staying healthy, or succeeding after a real transfer. Those outcomes require evidence this dataset does not contain.

How the dataset became a model

Six steps, with checks at every handoff.

The same overall process is used for each sport. Sport-specific statistics remain intact at the start, then are translated into a smaller set of clearly named comparison themes.

Collect and record

Download public research data, preserve its source, season and usage notes, and keep the raw inputs separate from derived files.

Build one season table

Give each row the same basic structure: sport, season, athlete, role, physical fields, opportunity and performance measures.

Define broad roles

Map league positions into defensible groups: for example NBA guard, forward and center, or NHL center, wing, defense and goalie.

Make seasons comparable

Rank performance within the same role and season. Small samples are pulled toward the group average so a short hot streak is not treated like a full year.

Train, then look forward

Models learn from seasons before 2025 and are checked on 2025. Earlier rolling checks are added when a role has enough history.

Publish with guardrails

The portal explains why it returned a result and can withhold a score when data, shared themes or evidence are too weak.

Data sourcing

Public sources, kept traceable by league.

The current build draws on 224 local source files. Each sport has its own adapter because a sack, steal, takeaway and tackle cannot simply be placed in one raw column and called equivalent.

NFL

nflverse

Seasonal rosters, participation and player statistics form the core. Licensing and provider obligations are tracked separately.

NBA

SportsDataverse / ESPN

Packaged player-season statistics and profiles support the local research build. Upstream redistribution rights still need review before public release.

NHL

MoneyPuck + NHL Stats

Performance and biography information provide skater and goalie coverage. MoneyPuck’s non-commercial and attribution terms remain important.

Premier League

Vaastav FPL + ESPN

Fantasy Premier League histories provide the main season panel; sparse profile enrichment supplies some physical information.

Release rule: a source with unresolved rights may support local research but should not silently enter a public or commercial model. Provenance is part of the model, not an afterthought.

Why use several seasons?

Performance changes yearly. Bodies are measured less often.

Five seasons give the project more athletes, more role examples and a genuine future season for evaluation. But repeating an undated height or weight beside several seasons does not turn it into five separate measurements.

Seasonal context matters

A 90th-percentile season means the player ranked highly against peers in that role and season—not against every athlete in every sport.

History tests stability

The project can compare a recent year with a sustained three-year view and can judge a forecast against a simple history-based baseline.

Physical timing stays honest

Current or undated profiles are marked as such. They are useful for approximate build comparison but cannot support claims about annual body change.
