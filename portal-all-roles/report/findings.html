Data and model findings · snapshot dated 21 October 2026

Four leagues, four very different evidence bases.

The project has enough data to describe broad roles and surface useful comparisons. It does not have equally complete measurements—or equally strong models—in every sport.

Compare the leagues See model evidence
The headline finding

Same-sport performance context is the most dependable part of the current project. Physical-only and cross-sport scores are useful for exploration, but the evidence is not yet strong enough to treat them as predictions of real transfer success.

Exploratory data analysis

Six observations shaped the design.

The most useful EDA findings were not flashy correlations. They were practical warnings about unequal samples, opportunity and what a missing value should mean.

69.4%of all rows have an observed descriptive score
12,016rows are eligible as supervised model labels
115role-and-season cohorts were scored
20cohorts were intentionally skipped
Where every row went: 12,016 eligible labels + 7,181 low-sample descriptive rows = 19,197 scored rows. The remainder are 5,311 not observed, 2,681 source/label abstentions, 249 unregistered generic NFL rows and 214 long-snapper rows with no public performance label. The 20 skipped cohorts are five seasons each for NFL offensive tackle, interior line, long snapper and unresolved generic roles.
1

Role sizes vary widely

The snapshot ranges from 2,017 NFL wide-receiver seasons to 214 long-snapper seasons; NBA guards have 1,325 seasons while centers have 403. One confidence rule cannot fit every group equally well.

2

Playing time changes meaning

Counts such as tackles, steals or recoveries depend on opportunity. The pipeline records availability, uses role-appropriate exposure and pulls tiny samples toward the group average.

3

Present is not the same as dated

Almost complete height and weight coverage can still come from current profile pages. That is enough for approximate body comparison, not a history of how a body changed.

4

Broad roles are more honest

Public data supports broad groups better than precise tactical jobs. Fine assignments need lineup, tracking or coaching context that the current panel does not contain.

5

No score is not a zero

A missing or withheld result can mean low opportunity, missing physical fields, too little shared information or an unsupported target role. It does not mean poor ability.

6

Context beats one giant ranking

The useful unit is a percentile within the same role and season. Low-sample shrinkage also pulls many descriptive scores toward the middle. A 90 for an NFL edge rusher and a 90 for an NHL defender still describe different peer groups.

What was modeled

Four views, each answering a different question.

The project deliberately keeps observed results, forecasts, physical resemblance and cross-sport analogy apart. Combining them into one unexplained number would hide where the evidence comes from.

Descriptive

Observed native rank

How strong was the player’s measured season compared with athletes in the same broad role and season?

Experimental

Native forecast

Given prior seasons, what might next season’s same-role percentile look like? It is checked against simple recent-history baselines.

Associational

Physical estimate

How is listed height, weight, body-mass index and age associated with the target role’s performance labels?

Exploratory

Transfer guesstimate

How does the athlete’s build and shared performance profile resemble people in another role? It is an analogy, not a transfer probability.

Model choice in plain words: each fitted head uses a simple regularized line model. It is intentionally easier to inspect and harder to overfit than a complex black box. Several strength settings are tried, and the project keeps the one that performs best within training data.

Frozen model evidence

Fitting a model is not the same as validating a claim.

Models train on seasons before 2025 and are tested on the held-out 2025 season. Errors, rank agreement and performance against simple baselines are recorded before a result is shown.

23/26 registered role-based physical heads could be fitted
4 physical heads cleared the current full evidence gate: NFL edge, NBA guard, NBA forward and EPL midfielder
22/26 native same-role forecast heads could be fitted
272 cross-sport bridge heads were fitted; none is direct evidence of real transfer success

What passed

Twelve native forecast heads cleared the project’s internal evidence gate. Only four physical heads passed the strict 90% bootstrap gate:

  • NFL edge: error 23.73 vs 25.00 neutral (182 tests)
  • NBA guard: 23.94 vs 25.00 (189)
  • NBA forward: 22.31 vs 25.00 (137)
  • Premier League midfielder: 23.34 vs 25.29 (123)

No NHL physical head passed. The practical lesson is that build alone is weak evidence.

What remains experimental

All deployed native forecasts are still labeled experimental under the stricter full-deployment standard. Bridge models reconstruct target-role percentiles; they do not validate what happens when a real athlete changes sports.
