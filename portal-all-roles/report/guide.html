Features, assumptions and limits · snapshot dated 21 October 2026

A score is a clue, not a verdict.

The portal is most useful when each result is read as a specific comparison: what was measured, who the peer group was and why the model was willing—or unwilling—to answer.

Learn the outputs See appropriate uses
The 20-second reading rule

First identify the output type. Then check the target sport and role, evidence label, data-availability note and explanation.

Never interpret a transfer guesstimate as “this player has an X% chance of succeeding.”

The outputs

Five labels that should not be mixed together.

Four outputs carry a 0–100-style result. Body fit is a separate description of how typical a listed build is for the target role; it is not a performance prediction.

Diagnostic

Body fit

A

How central or unusual the listed build is for that role.

A typical body can be useful context, but it is not evidence of skill. An unusual body can be a real outlier, not a mistake.

No score is not a zero

The project withholds results when required information is missing, too few meaningful themes overlap, the source role is ineligible, the athlete is too far outside known data, or the transfer view fails its required target-role check.

A physical or next-season result with weaker evidence may still appear, but it is clearly labeled experimental or unsupported.

“Unavailable” means the model should not answer—not that the player lacks ability.

What enters each model

Features are grouped by the claim they can support.

The project does not insert every available statistic into one model. Each feature family has a clear job, and target-role percentiles are created before sports are compared.

Assumptions

What must be roughly true for the comparison to help.

Assumptions are not hidden defects; they are choices to test. The portal is designed to expose them so a reader can disagree, refine a role or request better evidence.

Broad roles are stable enough

Guard, center, edge or midfielder can group athletes with reasonably related responsibilities, even though each label still contains tactical variation.

Opportunity defines a fair peer

Minutes, games, snaps and role-specific chances are good enough to distinguish a meaningful season from a tiny appearance.

Small samples deserve caution

Low-opportunity results should move toward the role-season average rather than be treated as fully reliable extremes.

Percentiles reduce drift

Comparing within the same role and season helps with changing eras, providers and league environments—but does not make every sport identical.

Shared themes can form analogies

Defensive disruption or progression can be meaningfully named in several sports, while still representing different actions and techniques.

Target patterns show resemblance

A model can reconstruct patterns seen in a target role well enough to create a hypothesis score. That is not evidence of actual adaptation.

Limitations

The largest gap is the outcome we ultimately care about.

There is no large, representative set of elite athletes who actually changed between these four sports. Without that outcome, the project can validate native descriptions and target-role reconstruction—not real transfer success.

Selection and missing context

  • Everyone in the panel already survived elite selection, so the data does not represent the general athlete population.
  • Technique, coaching, team tactics, health, motivation and learning speed are not modeled.
  • Public summaries miss assignment-level context, matchup difficulty and many off-ball actions.

Measurement timing

  • NFL rosters attach physical listings to seasons, but their measurement date and protocol are unknown.
  • NBA, NHL and Premier League profiles are mainly current or undated and may repeat across historical seasons.
  • Repeated values improve row coverage; they do not provide body-change evidence.

Evaluation design

  • The frozen 2025 test is chronological, but it is not athlete-disjoint: some test athletes appear in training history.
  • A separate unseen-history slice helps, but prospective testing on new seasons and athletes is still needed.
  • Model clipping to 0–100 can hide how far an extreme prediction sits outside the known range.

Sport-specific gaps

  • Premier League build data is sparse and its roles are broad.
  • NFL tackle, interior line and long snapper performance heads abstain.
  • NBA lacks league-wide tracking, hustle and matchup detail; NHL lacks line, pair, special-team and assignment context.

Rights and reproducibility

  • Several providers allow local research but require attribution, non-commercial use or further review.
  • A technically reproducible model is not automatically cleared for public redistribution or commercial use.

Meaning across sports

  • A same-season percentile is relative to one peer group, not a universal talent unit.
  • “Possession security” or “defensive disruption” names a family resemblance, not an identical motor or cognitive task.

Appropriate use

Use it to begin a conversation, not end one.

A good use produces a testable follow-up question. A bad use turns a research analogy into a high-stakes judgment that the data was never designed to support.

✓Helpful uses

  • Explain a player’s standing within the same role and season.
  • Generate cross-sport scouting or research hypotheses.
  • Compare a listed build with the usual range for a target role.
  • Teach how peer groups, opportunity and assumptions affect a score.
  • Identify cases that deserve coach, video, tracking or physical-testing follow-up.

×Not valid for

  • Probability of changing sports, making a roster or reaching a performance level.
  • Contract, draft, betting, medical or employment decisions.
  • Claims that a body type causes success.
  • Direct comparison of raw tackles, steals, sacks or recoveries across sports.
  • Claims about reaction, awareness, intelligence or personality.

Improvement roadmap

The next gains should come from better evidence.

More complex algorithms are not the first priority. Better timed measurements, finer context and real outcomes would change what the project is capable of claiming.

  1. Build a dated physical panel.

    Add rights-cleared season-specific testing for length, speed, change of direction and other measures, with protocol and measurement dates.

  2. Add tactical context.

    Use licensed event, lineup and tracking data to distinguish finer roles, assignments, teammates and quality of opposition.

  3. Collect real transfer evidence.

    Study crossover athletes, controlled tests and structured coach assessments so cross-sport scores can be checked against actual outcomes.

  4. Define mental constructs before measuring them.

    Predeclare reliable tasks for perception, decision speed or anticipation rather than inferring “awareness” from unrelated box-score statistics.

  5. Validate outside the current sample.

    Run prospective future-season and athlete-disjoint evaluations, publish uncertainty and check performance separately for each role.

  6. Clear release rights, then increase complexity.

    Resolve redistribution and commercial terms first. Consider hierarchical or richer models only after the labels and validation design justify them.
